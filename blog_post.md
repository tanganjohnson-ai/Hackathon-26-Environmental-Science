# From the Bluff to the Bayou—and Beyond!

At SGX3’s FacultyHack@Gateways 2026, I had the opportunity to think differently about what environmental science can look like for students at Southern University and A&M College. I presented my poster, “Mapping Urban Heat & Environmental Resilience with Science Gateways from the Bluff to the Bayou,” and served as a paper judge at the Gateways 2026 conference in Washington, D.C.

My FacultyHack project reimagines UFOR 271 Environmental Science as a course-based undergraduate research experience. Students will investigate urban heat and environmental resilience in Baton Rouge and across South Louisiana. Using accessible science gateways, browser-based notebooks, and local data, they will move from discovering and mapping patterns to analyzing evidence and sharing findings with their communities. The goal is to make meaningful, authentic research possible even for students who are new to coding.

The hackathon helped me see that this approach can reach beyond one course. I am now inspired to apply what I learned from redesigning UFOR 271 to redesign UFOR 398 Environmental Ecology, giving students another opportunity to investigate Louisiana ecosystems through fieldwork, data, and community-centered research.

It also sparked an idea for an 1890 capacity-building grant that could strengthen environmental teaching, undergraduate research, and extension in our Urban Forestry, Environment, and Natural Resources department. I hope to build a pathway connecting classroom investigations to community partnerships and practical resilience solutions.

FacultyHack reminded me that powerful tools matter most when they help students ask better questions about the places they call home.
